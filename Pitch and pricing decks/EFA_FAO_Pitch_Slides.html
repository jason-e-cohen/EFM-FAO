Scope + data
Scoped to your institution: what we need, and what you get
Student-level data for the 3–5 most recent years, plus a data dictionary defining every variable and value

Institutional data

Student ID · source and date of each inquiry activity · application date · intended or actual term · final admission status · final enrollment status · program · transfer status · student level · athletics

Student characteristics

Residency · gender · race/ethnicity · age or date of birth · first-generation status · veteran or active-military status

Academic data

Undergraduate: high school GPA, SAT/ACT, transfer GPA · Graduate: undergraduate GPA, GMAT/GRE

Student financial data

FAFSA applied and verification status · income or expected family contribution · aid by type and source: federal grants, Pell, loans, institutional and private scholarships, work-study

Decide before we scope

How should records be grouped into funnel levels? Which variable holds final admission status, and how should each category be classified? For example, is “Applied Withdrawn” an accepted applicant or a denied one? Settling this up front keeps the timeline on track.

Standard scope

  • One student level and type: undergraduate or graduate; freshman or transfer
  • Live dashboard, executive-style report, regression output, and scheduled batch scoring of new records

Available as follow-on projects

  • Financial Aid Optimization (Phase 2)
  • An additional student level or type
  • Longitudinal or in-depth descriptive analysis; geographic analysis; predictive tools

Tailored to your institution type

  • Open-admissions community colleges, graduate enrollment processes, and other school types each get their own treatment
Responsible by design
Privacy: hashed student IDs, no names or contact details, and aggregate-only reporting. Small cells are suppressed by default, and each client sets its own policy for named, authenticated staff. Aid guardrails: recommendations stay within the range the evidence supports, no cuts to offers already communicated, and an equity audit with your approval before anything is final.
