# Enrollment Funnel Analysis + Financial Aid Optimization

*Frankel Higher Ed, Enrollment Strategy. Leadership pitch, converted from the nine-slide deck.*

---

## 1. One partnership from funnel insight to aid policy to live monitoring

Enrollment Funnel Analysis (EFA) and Financial Aid Optimization (FAO) work as one partnership in five connected steps.

**Know your exposure (market context).** Where enrollment and revenue depend on financing, including the new federal borrowing limits, by program and student group.

**Know your funnel (EFA).** Where prospects leave, what predicts movement, and what the incoming class will look like, with a range.

**Measure price response (FAO).** How much a dollar of aid changes enrollment for your students, established through study design.

**Set your aid policy (FAO).** A scholarship policy your staff can apply as offers go out, with budget, class-size, and discount-rate trade-offs in view.

**Steer the cycle (monitoring).** A live view of class, revenue, and spend against last year and plan, with melt risk flagged early.

The leadership answer is a defensible, documented response to "where should the next aid dollar go?" and an early warning when the class is off track. It runs on one data model and one secure dashboard, and is reviewed with your team at every step.

## 2. See exactly where students leave, and what moves them

*Five stages, from first inquiry to enrolled at census: inquiry, application, admit, deposit, and enrolled at census. Deposit to census is reported as its own stage, so melt is visible.*

**Conversion at every stage, by student group.** A fixed, published set of views by cohort and by segment such as residency, student type, source, and program. Small groups are suppressed by default; each client sets its own policy for named, authenticated staff.

**What predicts movement.** The characteristics and behaviors linked to applying and enrolling, with confidence intervals, clearly labeled as associations rather than causes.

**A forecast of the incoming class.** Built only from what was known at the student's decision date, checked against your most recent cohort, and shown with a range.

**Your definitions, agreed up front.** We settle how records map to funnel stages, such as how to treat applicants who withdrew, before any analysis begins.

## 3. We measure how students respond to price, by design

*The foundation of every aid recommendation.*

The question that matters is what an extra dollar of aid does to a student's decision to enroll. Aid is targeted for reasons of its own, so the answer is established through study design.

**Primary evidence: a short bonus-aid test.** A random share of eligible admits receive extra aid ($0, $2,000, or $4,000). Nobody receives less than they otherwise would. It is the cleanest answer to "what does a dollar do?"

**Supporting evidence: the thresholds in your award rules.** Where your merit grid steps up at a GPA or test cutoff, students just above and just below are comparable. We use it wherever it passes our diagnostic checks.

**Combined into one price-response anchor.** Each source is reported on its own, then combined. We flag it when they disagree.

**Used in one transparent model for every segment.** Simple where simplicity works, so every recommendation can be explained to your board and defended to counsel.

Three points shape how you start. Tier 1 uses your award rules alone, with a wider range. Tier 2 adds the bonus-aid test, approved by your enrollment and financial aid leaders. A standing test then narrows the estimate each year.

## 4. A scholarship policy your team can apply, with trade-offs in view

*The deliverable is award levels by group, not a list of individual awards.*

The recommended policy is a grid of award levels by academic index (fifths), need band (four bands plus No FAFSA), and residency (three groups), about 75 cells in all. Cells use policy attributes only.

**Applied as offers roll out.** Offers go out on a rolling basis, so a policy staff can use at packaging time works better than a list for a class not yet known. It exports as a policy document for counsel review.

**Built around your goals.** The policy maximizes expected multi-year net tuition revenue within your discretionary budget, with optional class-size and discount-rate limits.

**Scenarios you can compare.** Budget at 75%, 100%, and 125%; a headcount-maximizing option; and a class-size floor where it applies. Each shows class, revenue, spend, and discount rate.

**Every number comes with a range.** Each scenario is shown if students prove less, about as, or more price-sensitive than estimated, and the budget holds in all three.

Three guardrails apply throughout. Awards on communicated offers can only rise (revisions and renewals), within the range tested. No group's award falls more than a set amount (default $3,000). Athletes, waivers, and named scholarships keep their current packages.

## 5. Know your market, your exposure, and your price position

*Evidence that informs the decision, alongside the model.*

**Federal loan-limit exposure (Tier 1).** How many admits, by program, relied on PLUS loans in past cohorts, and how many dollars now sit above the new federal limits. We translate the gap into an enrollment and revenue range. This is most relevant for graduate and professional programs and for parent borrowing.

**Where admitted students went instead (add-on).** A National Student Clearinghouse match, joined to public net-price data, shows which institutions you lose students to and the net-cost gap by student group.

**What admitted students tell you (add-on).** A short survey of enrolling and non-enrolling admits captures the competing offers they held and the reasons behind their choice.

**Your price position against peers (add-on).** Sticker and net price by income band against a peer set you approve, plus what-if tests of a tuition change on the admitted pool. Tuition scenarios cover the admit stage only; they do not capture effects on who applies.

These views shape strategy and the conversation with your leadership. They stay descriptive: they inform the price model's context but never feed its estimates.

## 6. Steer the cycle with a live view of class, revenue, and spend

*Weekly or biweekly refresh through yield season.*

The dashboard has five views: funnel and pacing, scenario planner, policy grid, market context, and equity audit. Headline tiles cover admits, deposits, net tuition revenue, discount rate, discretionary spend, and melt risk by segment, each against last year and plan, with a projected census shown as a range. A methodology and lineage panel sits on every view.

**Pacing against last year and plan.** Admits, deposits, revenue, and discretionary spend against the same date last year and against your targets.

**A projected census, with a range.** Current counts by stage, combined with how past classes converted at the same point before the deadline.

**Melt flagged early.** Segment-level risk from signals known at deposit, such as housing application, verification, unmet need, and FAFSA status.

**The right view for each audience.** The enrollment team sees full detail. Leadership sees headline scenarios, the policy summary, and the equity audit. Student-level lists return only through your secure channel.

## 7. Built to be trusted by your board, your counsel, and your campus

*Aid decisions carry legal, equity, and reputational weight. The safeguards are part of the method.*

**Fair by design.** Awards follow policy, not personal traits. Race, ethnicity, and gender are never model inputs; they are used only to audit results. Policy groups use academic, need, and residency attributes only. We use no consumer credit data, and an equity audit accompanies every recommendation.

**Private by design.** Student records stay in a secure environment. Data arrive de-identified with hashed keys, with no names, contact details, or full ZIP codes. The dashboard shows aggregates only, groups under 10 students are suppressed by default (each client can set its own policy for named, authenticated staff), and raw files are deleted at engagement close.

**Honest by design.** These are numbers you can explain and defend. No number appears without its range or a stated reason it has none. Only information known at the decision date enters the models, and missing data is never treated as zero. Every figure traces to a run, a data version, and a code version.

**Reviewed by design.** Nothing is applied until you say so. You and your counsel review every recommendation before use. Each model release is tested on synthetic institutions where the right answer is known, and each engagement includes a backtest on your own most recent cohort. Publishing is always a deliberate human step.

## 8. A partnership that follows your enrollment calendar

*Phase 1, funnel analysis: 8–10 weeks. Phase 2, aid optimization: 4–6 weeks, following Phase 1.*

| Milestone | When | What happens |
|-----------|------|--------------|
| Kickoff and definitions | Start | Agree on goals, funnel stages, and data definitions |
| Data delivered and checked | Early in Phase 1 | De-identified files; every check and row count shared with you |
| Funnel analysis complete | Phase 1: 8–10 weeks | Where students leave and what moves them |
| Aid optimization begins | After Phase 1 (Tier 2) | Price evidence and aid modeling build on the funnel analysis |
| Aid policy recommendation | Phase 2: 4–6 weeks | Scenarios, policy grid, and equity audit, reviewed with you and counsel |
| In-cycle monitoring | First admit release | Weekly or biweekly through census |

**What you receive.** A live dashboard covering funnel, pacing, scenarios, policy grid, market context, and equity audit. A scholarship policy document with the recommended award levels by group, ready for staff to apply and counsel to review. An executive report of key findings and recommendations, delivered following the dashboard. In-cycle monitoring of pacing, projected census, and melt risk through yield season.

**Then it compounds.** Every cycle adds evidence. Award revisions are logged with date and reason, and a standing price test narrows the estimate each year, so next year's policy rests on more than this year's.

## 9. Start where you are, and grow into the full partnership

*Two tiers, so start-up effort matches what you need.*

### Tier 1: Foundation

**What you send:**

- Three entering cohorts
- Final aid packages by component, with offer dates where you have them
- Your merit grid

**What it supports:**

- Funnel analysis and yield forecast
- In-cycle pacing and melt monitoring
- Federal loan-limit exposure
- A first estimate of price sensitivity from your award rules, with a wider range

### Tier 2: Full partnership

**What you send:**

- Four to five entering cohorts
- Every package version, with its date
- Merit grid with effective dates, and approval for a price test

**What it adds:**

- Everything in Tier 1
- A recommended scholarship policy grid with budget scenarios and an equity audit
- A price estimate anchored in design-based evidence

### Optional additions

In-cycle export (powers weekly pacing and melt), a Clearinghouse match (where admits went), an admitted-student survey (competing offers and reasons), a peer set (net-price comparison), and neighborhood context (public Census indicators you append before de-identifying).

### Next step

A kickoff conversation to agree on definitions, choose a tier, and scope a pilot cycle. Undergraduate first-time and transfer cohorts are in scope now; graduate and professional programs are included in the funnel, pacing, and loan-limit views.
