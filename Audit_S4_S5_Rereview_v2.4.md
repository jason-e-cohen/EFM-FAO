## 1. Verdict on the review

**Holds with minor revisions.** Both Critical conclusions stand. The Richmond screen verdict (**Not recommended**) stands at the review's ±3% class band under every pool, prior, target and bonus type tested, but it is **not robust to the class band**: at ±10% a realistic five-band pool gives Optional or Recommend. Three conclusions change in substance: (4) the all-eligible analysis does not remove a "+6% estimand bias" (that bias is an evaluation-point artifact that disappears for every design once the AME is computed at status-quo awards, as v2.5 already specifies), the precision gain is about 20% in logit units rather than 34%, and the analysis is fragile to any staff behaviour that differs by test status (±0.10 logit → ±20% slope bias, undetectable by the proposed compliance check). (3) The claim that a median-position target could turn the verdict positive is not supported: at the review's own guardrail a raise-the-median target is infeasible for aid alone. The screen's EVSI under the production rule can be **negative** (−$120k in the five-band base case), which the review did not anticipate. The §8 median constraint cannot be implemented as written. The 509 conditional-scholarship lag is one year, not two, and two data files carry stale column labels.

**Cost model (1.3).** For a hard cap, cost at zero effect understates: every enrolled test student carries the bonus, induced ones included, so the prior-mean cost is $375k, not $315k (+19% at D = $4,000; the gap grows with D and θ). For a ±3% *band* the review's value model credits induced enrollees with net tuition, so charging the test their full bonus with no offset is inconsistent with it; a one-cycle $4,000 test at θ = 1.0 induces about 5 enrollees, 3.8% of the class, which itself breaches the band. For an uncapped program the prior-expected net cost is right in principle but is negative at the prior mean (the bonus roughly pays for itself near break-even), which makes the ratio gate undefined; the review's own uncapped figures quietly used the zero-effect cost. At N = 650 the verdict at ±3% is the same under every cost treatment, so none of this changes the Richmond answer; it changes the ±5% answers (Optional under net cost).

**Decision-model logic (1.4).** EVPI is computed correctly relative to the no-test choice when that choice is the status quo (true in the two-band pool, a0 = (0, 0)); the formula subtracts V0 in `voi2.py` but **not** in `evpi.py`, which is correct only because a0 = (0, 0) there. In the five-band pool the prior choice is a reallocation, and the review's `evpi.py` formula would overstate EVPI by V0 ($0.93M over three cohorts); `voi5.py` subtracts it. EVSI ≤ EVPI holds in every run. EVSI ≥ 0 does **not** (A5): the quantile-feasibility rule takes more risk as the posterior narrows, and when scored at the true slope that risk costs more than the information is worth. An unconstrained Bayes rule on the same pool gives EVSI +$34k (D = $4,000 × 1) and +$282k ($8,000 × 2), confirming the machinery. Both terms must be scored by the same function (I used raw revenue, as the review did); otherwise EVPI can go negative as well.

**When would a test be Recommend or Optional for Richmond?** On the evidence here, only when the class band is wide enough for the grid to act on what the test learns: at ±10% (five-band pools, revenue objective, base prior) the verdict is Recommend or Optional with a renewable bonus and Recommend with a one-year bonus; at ±5% it is Not recommended under the capped cost and Optional under the net cost, i.e. a judgment call on the cost model; at ±3% it is Not recommended under every pool, prior, target, rule and bonus type I tried, including the review's own prior sensitivity range. A wide prior (σ0 = 1.0) brings the ±3% case to within $20k of zero. A median-position target does not help: at the 5%-of-tuition guardrail aid cannot move Richmond's enrolled median, so the target shrinks the admissible set and the test has nothing to decide. The one question that decides the screen is therefore the review's open question 6 (capacity band), not question 1 (objective).

### Task 4. Estimand and pooling

1. **Reproduced** unchanged: SDs 0.548 / 0.438 / 0.391 / 0.287; θ = 1.307 at status quo, 1.488 at bonus-inclusive awards (n = 4,843; `chk.py`: 1.488 recomputed exactly, 1.314 at base gifts for the test population).
2. **Pooling logit slopes:** right under the one-slope model; it also makes the disagreement flag invariant to p(1 − p) composition. If the local slope differs, the pooled estimate is biased by (RD weight) × (difference), about 14% of the difference at the synthetic SEs; the 1.5-SE flag detects only differences above roughly 70% of the slope. Report the weight and cap it (A23).
3. **ITT on all eligibles:** valid only if the non-test eligibles are exchangeable with the test $0 arm. A ±0.10 logit test-status effect biases the all-eligible slope by ±20% while leaving test-only unbiased; the test-$0-versus-non-test compliance contrast has SE ≈ 1.5 pp and cannot detect it. Compliance check that would work: not a yield contrast but a *process* contrast, i.e. other discretionary aid, revision rates and reconsideration rates by test status, and blinding of staff to membership. Where blinding is impossible, the all-eligible estimate is secondary (A22).
4. **Replacement conversion test:** meaningful (it tests p\* calibration where the v2.4 identity could not) but under-powered at the 5% tolerance against the review's own 6.6% level bias (A24). Evaluating the AME at status-quo awards, which v2.5 already specifies, is the change that actually fixed the 1.31 versus 1.49 ambiguity (A20).
